1. Let x0=0, x1=1, x2=2, and let y0=1, y1=3, y2=7. Write down the Lagrange polynomials ℓ0, ℓ1, ℓ2 associated with the points {x0,x1,x2}. Write the formula for the interpolating polynomial p∈ℙ2 satisfying p⁢(xj)=yj for each j. (You do not need to expand p.)

Solution. Using ℓj⁢(x)=∏k≠jx−xkxj−xk,

ℓ0⁢(x) =(x−1)⁢(x−2)(0−1)⁢(0−2)=(x−1)⁢(x−2)2,
ℓ1⁢(x) =(x−0)⁢(x−2)(1−0)⁢(1−2)=−x⁢(x−2),
ℓ2⁢(x) =(x−0)⁢(x−1)(2−0)⁢(2−1)=x⁢(x−1)2.

The interpolating polynomial is

p⁢(x)=y0⁢ℓ0⁢(x)+y1⁢ℓ1⁢(x)+y2⁢ℓ2⁢(x)=(x−1)⁢(x−2)2−3⁢x⁢(x−2)+7⁢x⁢(x−1)2.

(Expanding, p⁢(x)=x2+x+1, which indeed gives p⁢(0)=1, p⁢(1)=3, p⁢(2)=7).

2.

  1. (a)

    Which of the following are linear functionals on ℙn? Give brief justifications.

    ϕ⁢(p)=p⁢(0)+p′⁢(1),ψ⁢(p)=p⁢(0)+1,χ⁢(p)=(p⁢(0))2.
  2. (b)

    Let ϕ0⁢(p)=p⁢(0), ϕ1⁢(p)=p′⁢(0), and ϕ2⁢(p)=p⁢(1) for p∈ℙ2. Show that the only p∈ℙ2 with ϕ0⁢(p)=ϕ1⁢(p)=ϕ2⁢(p)=0 is p≡0. (Hint: what are the roots, with multiplicity, of such a p?) Do these functionals specify a unique polynomial?

Solution.

(a)

  • •

    ϕ is linear by linearity of evaluation and linearity of the derivative.

  • •

    ψ is not linear, since ϕ⁢(0)=1≠0.

  • •

    χ is not linear, since taking p≡1, we have χ⁢(2⁢p)=4, but 2⁢χ⁢(p)=2.

(b) Suppose p∈ℙ2 satisfies p⁢(0)=p′⁢(0)=0 and p⁢(1)=0. Since p⁢(0)=p′⁢(0)=0, r=0 is a root of p of multiplicity at least 2, and since p⁢(1)=0, r=1 is another root. So p has at least 3 roots, counting multiplicities. Since deg⁡(p)≤2, the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra implies p≡0.

(Alternatively, write p⁢(x)=a0+a1⁢x+a2⁢x2. Then p⁢(0)=a0=0 and p′⁢(0)=a1=0, so p⁢(1)=a2=0.)

By Lemma 3.4, for any numbers y0,y1,y2, there is a unique p∈ℙ2 with p⁢(0)=y0, p′⁢(0)=y1, and p⁢(1)=y2. (Explicitly, p⁢(x)=y0+y1⁢x+(y2−y0−y1)⁢x2.)
